# Internal review report

Use this default for `review`, `standard review`, and `deep review`; honor a requested alternative format. For quick review keep approximately 3–7 consequential findings, compress the sections, and disclose uninspected audits rather than filling them with low-value comments. A scope-limited request may use a focused report and must not imply a whole-paper assessment.

Start with `# ACL/ARR Internal Review` and a short coverage note naming mode, inspected materials, unavailable evidence, and policy verification status/source dates. Give findings stable IDs, discuss each fully once, and refer to those IDs from later audits and the revision plan instead of repeating the critique. Scale detail to manuscript size and requested depth. Use the following numbered sections:

1. **Paper Summary:** Research question, approach, contribution, evidence, and main conclusion as a reviewer would understand them.
2. **Overall Assessment:** Strongest evidenced reason to accept, strongest reason to reject or unresolved concern, and internal readiness. If no substantive reason on one side is established, say so. Choose `READY`, `NEARLY READY`, `MAJOR REVISION`, or `NOT READY`; explain what drives it. These are author-facing labels, not official outcomes. If essential checks are unavailable, mark readiness provisional; do not present unchecked compliance as READY. Omit whole-paper readiness for a scoped review.
3. **ARR-style Assessment:** Applicable current dimensions, short justification for each, and confidence with its evidence and limits. Use verified official numeric scales or qualitative judgments as directed by the policy workflow.
4. **Strengths:** Substantive, supported strengths only.
5. **Major Issues:** `BLOCKER` and `MAJOR` findings. For each give severity, location, why it matters, evidence, and concrete recommended action.
6. **Minor Issues:** `MODERATE`, `MINOR`, and useful `OPTIONAL` findings, explicitly distinguished; the heading does not downgrade MODERATE severity. Use the same finding fields and avoid duplicate issues.
7. **Claim–Evidence Audit:** Important claims with evidence locators and directly supported / partially supported / unsupported / cannot verify judgments; use the experimental reference's table when helpful.
8. **Experimental & Statistical Audit:** Design, baseline fairness, metric validity, statistical support, checkpoint/model selection, and consequential confounds. If nonexperimental, state applicability rather than inventing experiments.
9. **Reproducibility Audit:** Subheadings `Sufficiently specified`, `Missing`, and `Ambiguous`; distinguish paper-level omissions from appendix/supplement/repository information. Include deep-review evidence statuses when tracing claims.
10. **Citation / Related Work Audit:** Coverage, correctness where inspected, novelty positioning, and verified missing critical related work; state uncertainty.
11. **Limitations / Responsible NLP Audit:** Meaningful limitations and relevant impact/ethical/disclosure concerns, distinguishing their kind and evidence.
12. **Dataset / Software Audit:** Include only for meaningful introduced or promised artifact contributions; assess usefulness and usability. Omit this section when inapplicable and preserve the other section numbers.
13. **Submission / Desk-Rejection Risks:** Only genuine risks, separating verified official requirements from general advice or skill recommendations. If current policy or submission materials are unavailable, disclose the verification gap without manufacturing a risk. Say no genuine risk was identified only within the checked scope.
14. **Prioritized Revision Plan:** Group actions under `Must fix before submission`, `Strongly recommended`, and `Nice to have`. Link actions to findings, avoid duplicate experiments, and end with the **top 3 changes with the highest expected impact on the review outcome**. Give fewer if fewer supported changes exist; do not fabricate issues to fill a quota.

Apply the shared severity to issues across all sections. `PASS` is for a checked aspect, not a whole-paper certificate. Readiness, evidence status, and current ARR scores are separate judgments. Review completeness is also separate from scientific soundness.
